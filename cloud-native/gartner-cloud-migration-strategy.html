Gartner: Choosing the Right Approach for Application Modernization and Cloud Migration

14 slides · about 20 min · open the deck · JSON

This July 2024 supplementary note selects modernisation approaches by root cause. Diagnose the six business and IT drivers, identify whether technology, structure or functionality needs change, then compare rehost, replatform, rearchitect, rebuild and replace. Retain, encapsulate and retire remain valid alternatives; apply choices incrementally by component.

Big ideas

  • Five main approaches, in rising scope: rehost (lift and shift), replatform (lift and reshape), rearchitect (refactor), rebuild (rewrite), replace (buy COTS/SaaS).
  • Alternatives when modernizing isn't worth it: retain (leave as is), encapsulate (wrap in an API), retire (decommission).
  • Each approach changes different layers: rehost/replatform fix technology; rearchitect fixes technology and architecture; rebuild/replace can also change functionality.
  • Diagnose first using six drivers: business fit, innovation and agility (business); cost, complexity and risk (IT).
  • Map drivers to root cause: cost, complexity and risk point to technology; business fit and innovation point to functionality; agility and complexity point to architecture.
  • Pick the approach that fixes the causing layer with acceptable value, time to value, disruption, cost of change and risk; don't expect replatforming to improve business fit.
  • Modernize incrementally, component by component, not as a big bang; different components may need different approaches.

Why the choice of approach matters

States the note's key findings and recommendations, gives survey evidence that modernization spending is rising, and explains why picking the wrong approach is costly.

  • Pick the approach from the problem, not from fashion. Leaders often modernize or migrate without establishing why the app obstructs the business, so the chosen approach misses the root cause. Wrong choices lead to budget overruns, late delivery, little benefit, process disruption, unmaintainable code or a cancelled programme.
  • Portfolios decay, so modernization spend is rising. Apps lose business fit, innovation support and agility while getting costlier, more complex and riskier. In the 2023 CIO survey 46% planned more modernization spend, 50% more cloud-platform spend and 47% less legacy-infrastructure spend.
  • There is no one-size-fits-all fix. Gartner's method is a cause-effect analysis using six drivers, followed by an evaluation framework to choose per application.
  • Modernization is not always cloud migration. Most efforts involve cloud, but a COBOL app can be rearchitected to Java and stay on the mainframe, or replaced by an on-premises COTS package.
Application modernization
Improving an existing application's technology, architecture or functionality so it better supports the business.
COTS
Commercial off-the-shelf software bought from a vendor rather than built.

Check yourself

A CIO launches a 'move everything to cloud' modernisation programme because competitors are doing it. Eighteen months later, costs are up and the business sees no benefit. What did Gartner's note warn about?
Choosing an approach from fashion without first diagnosing why each application obstructs the business. The chosen approach then misses the root cause, which leads to overruns, late delivery, little benefit or a cancelled programme. The fix is a cause-effect analysis per application using the six drivers before picking an approach.
A bank's COBOL system must be modernised, but the mainframe contract runs for 8 more years. A teammate says modernisation is impossible until they can move to cloud. Do you agree?
No. Modernisation is not always cloud migration: the COBOL code can be rearchitected to Java and stay on the mainframe, or the app can be replaced by an on-premises COTS package. Pick the approach from the drivers, and treat the hosting location as a separate decision.
A board member cites a survey showing CIOs plan more spending on modernisation and cloud and less on legacy infrastructure, and asks to fund all applications equally. What is the flaw?
The trend explains why portfolios need attention, because apps lose fit and agility while growing costlier and riskier, but it doesn't say which apps need what. There is no one-size-fits-all fix, so fund by application after diagnosis. Some will need rebuilding, some only replatforming, and some should be retained or retired.

The five approaches and what each can change

Defines the five approaches, covers which application layers each can change, lists the three alternatives for legacy apps, and introduces the two diagnostic questions.

  • Five approaches change different scopes. Rehost moves infrastructure; replatform changes runtime with minimal compatibility edits; rearchitect restructures existing code while preserving behaviour; rebuild rewrites it, possibly like-for-like; replace adopts COTS or SaaS. Compare the affected layer before comparing implementation cost.
  • Match approach to layer. Rehost and replatform change technology only; rearchitect changes technology and architecture but has limited value for functional problems; rebuild and replace can change functions and features and also technology and architecture.
  • Three alternatives apply when modernization is not worth it, usually decided in a portfolio assessment. Retain: leave as is for now (too much effort or risk, or near retirement). Encapsulate: expose data and functions as services through an API while hiding the internals. Retire: decommission, often as part of a replace.
  • Rehost vs rebuild in one line: rehost is cheapest and least risky but changes almost nothing; rebuild offers the most flexibility but costs the most and carries the most risk.
Rehost
'Lift and shift': redeploy to other infrastructure without changing code or features.
Replatform
'Lift and reshape': move to a new runtime platform with minimal code changes for compatibility, but no structural or feature changes.
Rearchitect
Re-engineer/refactor: restructure the code without changing external behaviour, to remove technical debt and improve non-functional qualities.
Encapsulate
Wrap a legacy app's data and functions behind an API so they can be reused without changing the internals.
Retain / Retire
Retain: leave as is for now. Retire: decommission the application.

Check yourself

An app's code and features are fine, but it runs on middleware that is going out of support. Which approach fits, and why not rebuild?
Replatform: the problem is in the technology layer, which replatforming changes with minimal code edits. Rebuilding would also change architecture and functionality that don't need fixing, adding cost, time and risk for no extra benefit.
A legacy claims engine is valuable and stable, but changing it is very risky, and a new mobile app needs its data. Modernise or one of the alternatives?
Encapsulate: expose its data and functions as services through an API, so the mobile app can use them while the implementation stays hidden and untouched. It extends the app's value cheaply. The engine's limits remain, so revisit it later if business fit or agility becomes the driver.
A team proposes rearchitecting an app into microservices because 'users say it is missing features'. Will rearchitecting fix that?
Probably not. Rearchitecting changes technology and architecture while preserving behaviour, so it has limited value for functional problems. Missing features point to functionality, which rebuild or replace can change. Rearchitect only if the real complaint turns out to be the speed or risk of adding features, which is an agility problem.

Six drivers, root cause and the selection framework

Describes the three business and three IT drivers of modernization, maps them to the application layer most likely causing the problem, and presents Gartner's framework rating each approach.

  • Define the issue with six drivers. Business: business fit (no longer meets requirements), innovation (blocks new opportunities), agility (cannot keep pace with change at acceptable cost and risk). IT: cost (too high relative to value), complexity (hurts maintainability and the cost and risk of change), risk (security, compliance, supportability, scalability, skills shortage on old platforms). Usually a mix of both tips the decision.
  • Drivers suggest a cause; investigate before selecting. Technology often contributes to cost, complexity or risk; functionality to business fit or innovation; architecture to agility or complexity. These are probable links, not a one-to-one diagnosis. Evidence may reveal more than one layer.
  • Then choose the approach that can change that layer. Technology faults: rehost, replatform or rearchitect. Architecture faults: rearchitect (or rebuild). Functionality faults: rebuild or replace. Gartner's framework also rates each approach on value, time to value, disruption, cost of change and risk: rehost is low on all of them, rebuild is the most flexible but highest cost and risk, and replace has high value with the greatest business disruption.
Six drivers of modernization
Business fit, innovation, agility (business) plus cost, complexity, risk (IT).
Technical debt
Accumulated shortcuts and outdated design that make an application costly and risky to change.

Check yourself

A system can't support a new digital sales channel. What must you diagnose before choosing an approach?
Whether the missing piece is business functionality or access to existing functions. If functionality is missing, rebuild or replace the component that needs it. If the functions exist but can't be reached by the new channel, encapsulating them behind APIs or integrating at the architecture level may be enough. Rehosting alone won't add the capability.
Releases of a core app take months and often break unrelated features. Rearchitect or rebuild?
Rearchitect is the likely fit. Slow, risky change points to agility and complexity, which usually come from the architecture, and rearchitecting fixes architecture and technology at lower cost, risk and disruption than a rebuild. Rebuild is justified only if the functionality must also change or the code can't be restructured.
Running costs of an app are too high for its value, but users are happy with it. Which drivers and layer does this point to, and which approaches would you shortlist?
The IT cost driver, most likely rooted in technology, such as expensive licences or over-provisioned infrastructure. Shortlist rehost or replatform, which are low on cost, risk and disruption. Rebuild or replace would add disruption and risk to fix functionality that already works. Check the evidence, because drivers suggest a cause but don't diagnose it one-to-one.
A sponsor prefers replacing an in-house HR system with SaaS because it has 'the highest value'. What does the framework add to that view?
Replace can change all layers and offers high value, but it causes the greatest business disruption: processes are redesigned and users retrained. Rehost would be low on value, cost, risk and disruption but would change little. Choose replace only if business fit or innovation is the driver and the business accepts the disruption.

Weighing consequences and the incremental approach

Lists the considerations to weigh when shortlisting approaches and recommends modernizing component by component.

  • Weigh five consequences before committing: potential business value (often overestimated, e.g. expecting a replatform to improve business fit), time to value, business disruption (retraining, process redesign; SaaS replacement is high), cost of change, and execution risk.
  • Agree scope with stakeholders. Shortlist approaches that address the actual problem, then compare outcomes, disruption, time, cost and execution risk. Choose a proportionate change rather than the most extensive one.
  • Modernize incrementally. The target can be a component rather than a whole app; Gartner recommends going component by component rather than big bang, and different components may need different approaches (some a rebuild, others just a replatform).
Time to value
How long before the modernization starts delivering business benefit.
Incremental modernization
Modernizing an application component by component rather than all at once (big bang).
Replace ('drop and shop')
Retire the app and repurchase the capability as COTS or SaaS.

Check yourself

A large app needs a new customer workflow, but its stable reporting module only needs a supported runtime. Must the whole app be rebuilt?
No. Gartner recommends modernising component by component. Rebuild or replace the workflow component, where functionality must change, and replatform the reporting module, where only the runtime is the problem. Confirm dependencies and sequence the work so each change delivers its benefit and risk stays contained.
A sponsor expects replatforming the ERP to make it fit the business better. How do you respond?
Replatforming changes only the technology layer, so it won't improve business fit, which is a functionality problem. Gartner notes that potential value is often overestimated in this way. Set expectations: if business fit is the driver, rebuild or replace is needed, with their higher cost, risk and disruption.
Two approaches both fix an app's diagnosed problem: replatform (3 months) and rebuild (14 months, more flexibility). The business wants value this year. Which, and why?
Replatform: it fixes the actual problem with shorter time to value, lower cost of change and less execution risk. Choose a proportionate change, not the most extensive one. Rebuild's extra flexibility is worth it only if a near-term need for functional change is expected.

Exam-style questions

Answer aloud or on paper before opening.

A government agency's 20-year-old COBOL benefits system costs too much to run, is hard to change, and its COBOL staff retire within 3 years. The benefit rules themselves still meet policy. Recommend a modernisation approach using the drivers and root-cause layers.
The drivers are cost, complexity, agility and risk (a skills shortage on an old platform), all pointing to technology and architecture rather than functionality, since the rules still fit. Rearchitecting, for example to Java, preserves behaviour while addressing these problems, possibly staying on the current platform. Rebuild is justified only if restructuring isn't viable. Modernise component by component, encapsulate stable parts behind APIs where new channels need them, and compare time to value, cost, risk and disruption before committing. The skills deadline sets the order of the work.
An on-premises web application has three complaints: hardware is ageing, the database needs constant hand-tuning, and features take months to ship. Compare rehost, replatform and rearchitect for moving it to cloud, and say which complaint each one fixes.
Rehost (lift and shift onto cloud VMs) fixes the ageing hardware quickly, cheaply and with the least risk, but changes nothing else. Replatform, for example moving to a managed database or managed runtime with minimal code changes, also fixes database operations and gains some managed-service benefits. Rearchitect, for example refactoring into services, is the only one that addresses slow feature delivery, which is an architecture and agility problem, at higher cost, time and risk. A sensible plan is to replatform now and rearchitect the slowest-changing components later.
A company wants to replace its 12-year-old custom CRM with a SaaS product. The sales team is attached to custom workflows. Using Gartner's framework, what should it consider before deciding?
Check that the drivers are business fit or innovation, because replace is justified for functional gaps, not for technology problems that replatforming could fix more cheaply. Replace offers high value but the greatest business disruption: sales processes must be redesigned and users retrained, and custom workflows may be lost. Weigh time to value, cost and execution risk with stakeholders. Plan data migration and the retirement of the old CRM.
Describe a structured method for selecting modernisation approaches across a 150-application portfolio, and show how it would avoid the failures Gartner describes.
First run a portfolio assessment to decide whether each app is retained, retired, encapsulated or modernised. For candidates, define the issues with the six drivers and trace each to its likely root-cause layer (technology, architecture or functionality), checking the evidence. Shortlist approaches that can change that layer, then weigh value, time to value, disruption, cost and risk with stakeholders. Execute incrementally per component. This avoids fashion-driven choices that miss the root cause, which cause overruns and little benefit, and it avoids big-bang rewrites.

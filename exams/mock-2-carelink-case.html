Background

Hospitals in the region discharge patients earlier than before, and many chronic patients would rather be monitored at home than readmitted. A regional hospital cluster has commissioned CareLink, a remote patient-monitoring platform. Patients wear a clip-on sensor that measures heart rate, blood oxygen (SpO2), temperature and motion; the patient's phone relays the readings. Nurses at a central monitoring hub watch dashboards and respond to alerts, and doctors run video teleconsultations.

The cluster's Electronic Medical Record (EMR) system and pharmacy dispensing system are 15 years old, run on premises in the hospital data centre and will not move in the next three years.

You are the Lead Solution Architect. Phase 1 covers monitoring, alerting and teleconsultation. Phase 2 adds deterioration risk scoring and GenAI-drafted consultation notes.

1.Phase 1 of the Platform

Actors:

  1. Patient: enrolled by a clinic; uses the CareLink app and wears a sensor.
  2. Caregiver: a family member the patient allows to see their status.
  3. Nurse: monitors assigned patients from the monitoring hub and acknowledges alerts.
  4. Doctor: reviews history, runs teleconsultations and issues e-prescriptions.
  5. Pharmacist: dispenses e-prescriptions through the hospital pharmacy system.
  6. Clinic Admin / Platform Admin: enrol patients, assign nurses and manage users.
  7. Sensor: the wearable device, paired to the patient's phone over Bluetooth Low Energy.
  8. EMR, Pharmacy System: on-premises hospital systems. National Digital Identity: OpenID Connect provider for patients and staff.
Use CaseDescription
Enrol PatientThe Clinic Admin enrols a patient, links the EMR record and assigns a nurse team.
Pair SensorThe Patient pairs a sensor with the app.
Stream VitalsThe sensor samples every 5 seconds; the app uploads readings in batches every 30 seconds and buffers them while offline.
Raise AlertWhen a reading breaches a rule (e.g. SpO2 below 92% for 60 seconds) the platform raises an alert to the assigned nurse.
Acknowledge AlertThe Nurse acknowledges the alert and records the action taken.
View VitalsNurses and Doctors view a patient's vitals chart for any window in the last two years; Patients and Caregivers view the last 7 days.
Book / Conduct TeleconsultationThe Patient books a slot; the Doctor and Patient meet by video.
Issue e-PrescriptionThe Doctor issues a prescription that is sent to the hospital pharmacy system.
Sync with EMREncounter summaries and alerts are written to the EMR; allergies and medications are read from it.

2.Phase 2 of the Platform

Use CaseDescription
Score Deterioration RiskA model scores each patient's risk of deterioration every 15 minutes from recent vitals and EMR history.
Draft Consultation NoteAfter a teleconsultation the platform drafts a structured note from the transcript and the hospital's clinical guidelines; the Doctor edits and signs it before it is filed to the EMR.

3.Extensibility of the Platform

New vital signs (e.g. blood glucose, respiratory rate) and new sensor models must be addable without code changes in multiple services.

4.Performance, Scalability and Capacity

  • 20,000 enrolled patients; up to 12,000 wearing sensors concurrently today, growing linearly to 24,000 by the end of year 2.
  • Readings every 5 s; uploads in batches every 30 s; phones buffer up to 2 hours offline.
  • A rule breach must appear on the assigned nurse's console within 10 s for 99% of breaches.
  • 300 concurrent nurses, 150 doctors, up to 400 concurrent video consultations.
  • Vitals history for any window within the last 2 years in under 3 s for 90% of requests.
  • Monitoring and alerting 99.9% available; booking and history 99.5%.

5.Security and Compliance

  • All health data must be stored and processed in-country; the hospital controls the encryption keys.
  • Access only by clinicians with a care relationship to the patient; every access is audited.
  • National identity numbers must not appear in URLs or logs.
  • Clinical records are corrected, never deleted; corrections are audited.
